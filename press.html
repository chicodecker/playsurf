Made by a surfer

PlaySurf is an indie game, and it started with a long wait. Its creator is a surfer who spent decades hoping for a surf game that felt like the real thing: the drop, the bottom turn that turns into speed, the lip pitching overhead as you line up the barrel. That game never came. So the creator stopped waiting and made it.

It was made to be played at home, with a son and a wife who surf too. One screen passes from hand to hand in Party Surf sessions: four waves each, judges scoring every ride, and a champion at the end.

Knowing surfing from the water shaped every part of the game. Each move was ridden, felt and tuned again and again (the timing of a snap, the line through a cutback, the moment to brake and let the barrel catch you) until the riding flowed and had style. PlaySurf keeps growing the same way, wave by wave: today it has 18 real spots, from Pipeline and Jeffreys Bay to Restaurants in Fiji, Safi in Morocco and the Amazon's Pororoca.

And it is free, on purpose. The idea is to offer PlaySurf to everyone who wants to have fun, and to let people who live far from the beach feel what surfing is like: the drop, the speed off the bottom turn, the moment the lip throws over your head.

Long

PlaySurf, The Ultimate Surfing Game, is a free indie 3D surf game by Decker Games, made by a surfer, that runs in any modern web browser, on a computer, a phone or a tablet. Players paddle into real surf spots and ride them like a surfer: speed comes from the bottom turn, the lip pitches over their head in the barrel, and five judges score every wave. There are 18 waves: a perfect wave pool, the barrels of Pipeline, Cloud 9, Supertubos, Greenbush and Restaurants in Fiji, the long walls of Uluwatu, Jeffreys Bay, Skeleton Bay and Chicama, the Lower Trestles A-frame, Campeche in Brazil, the Amazon tidal bore (the Pororoca), an arctic peak at Þorlákshöfn, Iceland, Matadeiro and Rosa Norte in Santa Catarina, the three giant barrels of Ilha dos Lobos, off Torres, the biggest wave in the game, and Safi, in Morocco, a long right-hand point with one barrel after another. In Party Surf, 2 to 6 friends share one device, four waves each, and the judges crown a champion.

Every ride is different: rain, sun showers with rainbows, dawn sessions and night surfing under the floodlights change the light and the water. Seven boards, from the Pro Model shortboard to a 9'2 longboard and a big wave gun, ride differently, and the game rewards real surfing: bottom turns, snaps, roundhouse cutbacks, floaters, laybacks, carve 360s, airs and barrels, linked into combos.

PlaySurf is built with three.js and WebGL and plays with a keyboard, a gamepad or touch controls. It is free, with no account and no download: open playsurfgame.com and paddle out.

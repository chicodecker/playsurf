Skeleton Bay in PlaySurf

The real wave

Skeleton Bay is a sand point on the coast of Namibia, near the port town of Walvis Bay, where the Namib desert meets the Atlantic. When a big swell lines up along the sand spit, the wave runs left for around two kilometres, barrelling section after section, and it is known as one of the longest barrels in the world. Local bodyboarders rode it first, and the rest of the surfing world found out about it in the late 2000s, after a surf magazine's Google Earth contest. Conditions are harsh: cold water from the Benguela current, fog, wind, a strong current down the point, and a sandbar that changes shape from year to year.

Surfing Skeleton Bay in PlaySurf

In PlaySurf, Skeleton Bay is a long left along a desert sand point, about 60 seconds of riding, and the barrels keep coming: one section throws, then the next, then the next. The water is a cold green, there is haze in the air, and behind the flat sand of the point the Namib dunes rise ridge after golden ridge, with a wreck rusting on the beach. Your surfer wears a fullsuit here.

How to surf Skeleton Bay

  1. Stay high and keep your speed: the barrel sections come one after another.
  2. Hold Shift to let the lip catch you, then let go and race out before the next section.
  3. It is a long ride: keep linking barrels and turns all the way down the point.
